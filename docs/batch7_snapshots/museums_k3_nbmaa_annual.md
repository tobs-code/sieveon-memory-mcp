WHY I GIVE 

“Pablo Picasso once said, ‘any form of art is a form of power: it has impact, it can affect change—it can not only move us, but it can also make us move.’ The New Britain Museum of American Art is a welcoming haven where visitors of diverse backgrounds can engage in inspiring exhibits which help us reflect, challenge, and affirm beliefs. In doing so, the Museum can move us to new perspectives about our cultural past, our current community, and our dreams of an inclusive future. Furthermore, the numerous events appealing to all ages provide an opportunity for the people in the surrounding communities to socialize in a safe environment.” —Barbara Laurain, Member Our thanks to the following NBMAA supporters 

The acquisition of Harriet Cany Peale’s Kaaterskill Clove , 1858, was made possible through a generous multiyear grant from the Robert C. Vance Foundation. 

“Harriet Cany Peale’s Kaaterskill Clove is an extraordinary picture and we are thrilled it has found a home here at the Museum. Not only is it skillfully rendered, but the detail is such that some features of the landscape are still identifiable in photos of the site today,” says Stephanie Mayer Heydt, Director of Collections and Exhibitions. “The acquisition is also a landmark purchase for the Museum—as our first major landscape painting by a woman artist, Kaaterskill Clove offers a powerful complement to our existing strength in American landscape painting. It speaks directly to our mission to expand and diversify the collection in meaningful ways.” “We are profoundly grateful to the Vance Foundation for their generous support, which made this acquisition possible,” says Brett Abbott, Executive Director and CEO. “Their commitment allows us to honor underrecognized artists like Peale and ensure their important contributions to American art are preserved and celebrated for generations to come.” 

## ON THE COVER Dear Friends, This has been a year of remarkable milestones—moments that invite us to pause, reflect, and celebrate. Amid the steady push toward our goals, it is essential to contemplate the beauty shaped by the unwavering dedication of our staff, the steadfast trust of those who support and protect this cherished institution, and the community of visitors who return again and again for inspiration, wonder, and solace. We think of the children standing in our galleries, perhaps seeing art in person for the very first time, and of our city, which depends on us as a pillar of cultural vitality and economic enrichment, shaping the lives of neighbors while welcoming travelers from near and far. This year, together, we achieved some of the most meaningful milestones in the Museum’s history. 

Our exhibitions were bold and transformative. 

The Land Carries Our Ancestors from the National Gallery of Art placed us on a national stage, while a retrospective of Vik Muniz—the only New England venue—captivated audiences. Additional highlights included David Hockney and James Sellars, explorations of Shaker artistry, and Lure of the Sea , celebrating the Hamm Collection’s 10th anniversary. We also welcomed important national loans from the Whitney Museum of American Art and the National Gallery of Art. We published a five-year Strategic Plan that carries the voices of our visitors, supporters, artists, staff, and community. This plan not only positions the Museum for financial stability today but charts a course for dynamic growth in the years ahead. It reaffirms our dual identity as both a world-class art museum and a deeply rooted community resource. From this plan emerges a refreshed mission and vision that eloquently articulate our purpose, alongside a roadmap guiding every department, every project, and every team member toward shared long-term goals and our broader campus vision. For the first time in a long time, the entire institution—leadership, front-line staff, and collections care alike—is aligned, moving forward together with clarity, focus, and momentum. We are also proud to announce that the Museum has once again achieved accreditation by the American Alliance of Museums (AAM), the highest national recognition for museums in the United States. Of Connecticut’s estimated 700 museums, only 21 hold this distinction, including just 12 art museums. Nationally, of roughly 33,000 museums, fewer than 1,100 are accredited. This distinction affirms our commitment to excellence, accountability, and the highest professional standards, while underscoring our dedication to continuous improvement. To our community, it signifies that this Museum—your Museum— meets the most rigorous benchmarks of our field and remains a trusted steward of American art.

The year also saw the approval of a new Collections Development Plan, charting the path for the Museum’s holdings into the future. Already, this vision is transforming our galleries. We welcomed 86 new works into the collection—13 purchases and 73 gifts. Among these, several acquisitions were groundbreaking: Harriet Cany Peale’s Kaaterskill Clove (1858), the Museum’s first major landscape painting by a woman artist; Rogelio Báez Vega’s The Country Club from his Construct of a No-Country 

series, deepening our commitment to a more inclusive and expansive vision of American art; our first textile by a Native American artist; and many more. In addition, nearly every gallery on the first floor has been freshly re-installed, offering new opportunities to see, reflect, and connect. Simultaneously, our programs and partnerships have broadened access and deepened impact. Homeschool Days has emerged as one of our fastest-growing educational programs, while our partnership with the Art Bridges Foundation has multiplied opportunities to share our collection more widely. Through Visitor Services, we have focused intently on meeting visitors where they are, curating social programs that serve as points of entry for new audiences while sustaining the Museum’s vitality. These initiatives continue our tradition of being the most welcoming museum in the region. 

As we look ahead, we do so with profound gratitude—for the staff whose talent and dedication power every achievement, for the supporters whose generosity makes growth possible, and for the community whose presence animates this Museum every day. This year has demonstrated the extraordinary things we can accomplish together, and the years ahead promise even greater accomplishments and shared moments of inspiration. 

With deepest thanks and optimism,

6 ANNUAL REPORT FY 2024–25 FRITZ HORSTMAN: VALLEY & BLUE LIGHT SEPTEMBER 6, 2024–JULY 27, 2025 

Light and shadows, landscapes, voids, and reversals; the subjects of Fritz Horstman’s exhibition were simultaneously very much of the everyday world and also something less easily defined. This exhibition featured Horstman’s U-Shaped Valley sculptures and works from his series of Folded Cyanotypes ,which are at once sculptures, prints, and drawings that also fit comfortably into the history of cameraless photography. 

ARTISTS IN RESIDENCE 

Celebrating the power of movement and art, we launched an exciting new Artist in Residence program, presented in partnership with The Hartford Dance Collective and inspired by 

Modern Women: Visionary Artists . Every Saturday (January– May 2025), a choreographer from The Collective led a free movement workshop. Each month featured a different choreographer, who performed a short original solo dance piece inspired by a work in the exhibition. The residency culminated in a dance performance by all choreographers on June 5 and 7, 2025. Sponsored by the Art Bridges Foundation. 

VIK MUNIZ: EXTRA-ORDINARY OCTOBER 6, 2024–FEBRUARY 23, 2025 

This major mid-career retrospective canvassed more than twenty-five years of Muniz’s work to transform the ordinary into the extraordinary. Working with a dizzying array of unconventional materials, Muniz painstakingly builds tableaux before recording them with his camera. From a distance, the subject of each resulting photograph is discernible; up close, the work reveals a complex and surprising matrix through which it was assembled. Often quoting iconic images from popular culture and art history, Muniz’s works draw on our sense of collective memory while defying easy classification and mischievously engaging a viewer’s process of perception. This exhibition was co-organized by the Foundation for the Exhibition of Photography, Minneapolis/Paris/Lausanne, and the High Museum of Art, Atlanta in association with the New Britain Museum of American Art. At the NBMAA, this exhibition was made possible by Kenneth L. Boudreau and Judith W. OCTOBER 5, 2024 

Our 22nd Annual Art Party of the Year was a lovely night of groovy tunes by the one and only Nat Reeves, delicious food and specialty cocktails, and a silent auction of extraordinary artwork and experiences. DAVID HOCKNEY & JAMES SELLARS: HAPLOMATICS DECEMBER 13, 2024–SEPTEMBER 21, 2025 

Haplomatics explored the wild realm of pseudo-scientific fantasy in the visual textual and musical collaboration between the artist David Hockney and the composer James Sellars. Their work together in the late 1980s became a synergy of art, technology, and music that resulted in a multi-media masterpiece, the animated film Haplomatics . Drawn from the collection of the New Britain Museum of American Art, Hockney’s innovative xerographic prints were displayed alongside the complete film for its first public debut.

“ACROSS THE NATION” FROM THE NATIONAL GALLERY OF ART 

The New Britain Museum of American Art partnered with the National Gallery of Art for the National Gallery’s “Across the Nation” initiative, which brings key works of art to just 10 well-respected museums across the United States in celebration of America’s 250th birthday. As part of the program, the Museum received two loans, on view May 2025 through December 2026: Robert Seldon Duncanson’s Fruit Still Life (c. 1849) and Winslow Homer’s East Hampton Beach, Long Island (1874). BLUE GRASS, GREEN SKIES: AMERICAN IMPRESSIONISM AND REALISM FROM THE LOS ANGELES COUNTY MUSEUM OF ART 

JANUARY 24–MAY 18, 2025 

Impressionism’s influence was felt globally, but perhaps nowhere as profoundly or as long lasting as in the United States. Featuring works by artists Childe Hassam, Mary Cassatt, George Bellows, Maurice Prendergast, and more, Blue Grass, Green Skies: American Impressionism and Realism from the Los Angeles County Museum of Art highlighted the evolution of Impressionism’s loose brushwork and vibrant color palette into a distinctly American art. This exhibition was organized by the Los Angeles County Museum of Art. Generous support for this project provided by Art Bridges.

MASTERWORKS OF COLOR: SHAKER CRAFTS AND THE ART OF GEORGE CHAPLIN FEBRUARY 21, 2025–OCTOBER 5, 2025 

Joy in Shaker life was expressed through color. This exhibition featured wooden objects, large and small, and textiles with color that was applied up to two hundred years ago. Sharing the gallery were paintings by the artist George Chaplin. He had no direct connection to the Shakers, but his works are also an expression of the emotions that respond to color. Organized by consulting curator M. Stephen Miller, this exhibition was one in a series exploring Shaker craft in dialogue with contemporary art. 

LURE OF THE SEA: MASTERWORKS OF AMERICAN COASTAL ART MARCH 21–JULY 20, 2025 

Lure of the Sea: Masterworks of American Coastal Art traced nearly 200 years of artistic engagement with the maritime world. This exhibition was drawn from the collection of the New Britain Museum of American Art and commemorated the tenth anniversary of the Museum’s landmark gift of coastal and marine art by collectors and longstanding supporters, Charles J. and Irene Hamm. Featuring masterworks from their donation alongside other works in the Museum’s distinguished holdings, Lure of the Sea illuminated the American coast as a realm of boundless artistic and human pursuit. This exhibition was supported by the Bailey Family Fund For Special Exhibitions. In-kind support provided by Thomas Mach Interiors, Inc. 

WONDER STORIES: MASTERWORKS OF PULP ART ILLUSTRATION MAY 23, 2024–SEPTEMBER 14, 2025 

In 2013, collector Robert Lesser gifted his extraordinary collection of 200 pulp art illustrations to the New Britain Museum of American Art. Today, the Robert Lesser Collection represents the greatest assemblage of pulp art in this country, preserving the history of this exclusively American art form. WONDER STORIES presented a selection of pulp masterworks from the Museum’s celebrated collection. The imagery featured is a testament to the compelling narrative of the stories, as well as the imaginations of the artists, who transformed words into fantastical images. OUR MEMBERS 

The Museum’s mission of inspiring, educating, and connecting people through the power of American art is embodied through our Membership. We are continuously grateful for our strong membership base, which totaled more than 5,000 Members at the end of June 2025. Their support allows the Museum to reach even wider audiences, ensuring that these meaningful experiences will always be available to our community. And the active participation of our Members at classes, lectures, and social events makes everything we do at the Museum possible. 

JOE FIG: CONTEMPLATING VERMEER 

JUNE 6, 2025–JANUARY 11, 2026 

Drawing on his visit to the historic exhibition of Vermeer at the Rijksmuseum in 2023, Joe Fig created a new body of paintings expanding on his decade-long Contemplation series. Fig’s intricately detailed paintings in this exhibition pay homage to the seventeenth-century painter and reflect on the way people engage with artworks in public spaces. This exhibition is organized by Sarasota Art Museum of Ringling College of Art and Design, curated by Rangsook Yoon, Ph.D., senior curator, Sarasota Art Museum. Generous support for this installation has been provided by Cristin Tierney Gallery, New York.
