1.1.1  Advice Understanding Firm Heterogeneity, Impacts and Constraints on Research and Development In South Africa 

During the 2024/2025 financial year, the National Advisory Council on Innovation (NACI) completed a comprehensive study on firm-level research and development (R&D) in South Africa and provided advice to the Minister of Science, Technology, and Innovation (STI). The study utilised administrative tax data from the South African Revenue Services-National Treasury database and comparative data from global sources, including the World Development Indicators and Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD). Its primary objective was to investigate the underlying reasons for the persistent decline in business expenditure on R&D (BERD), which has contributed to South Africa’s gross expenditure on R&D (GERD) dwindling to 0.6% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in 2020 from 0.83% in 2008/09, a far cry from the 1.5% target outlined in the 2019 White Paper. The study’s findings revealed a concerning trend. BERD’s share of GERD plummeted from 57% to 30%, with real BERD halving due to a decline in the number of R&D firms, lower R&D intensity, and high exit rates, particularly among large manufacturing firms, which account for 74–79% of R&D spending. Despite these trends, R&D firms outperform their counterparts, exhibiting 18% higher productivity in manufacturing, agreater likelihood of exporting (with 62% higher export values), and employing more workers (95 vs. 43) at higher wages. However, South Africa’s R&D incentives are limited compared to other middle-income countries, with public support at a mere 0.018% of GDP and declining direct government funding since 2008. The study suggests that reversing this decline requires a multifaceted approach. To boost R&D in South Africa, several policy recommendations are proposed. First, improving the national investment climate is critical to enhancing R&D expenditure by addressing key supply constraints, such as electricity provision and transport. Furthermore, offering tax exemptions and holidays for R&D expenditure, as well as incentivising Intellectual Property (IP) sales through higher after-tax returns, could encourage R&D activities. Additionally, promoting the use of external knowhow can increase firm productivity and R&D expenditure. Accelerating reforms to the R&D tax incentive, including streamlining the application process and reducing turnaround times, is also vital. Moreover, targeted interventions, such as grants and cash transfers, can provide essential support to exporting firms and small businesses. Finally, leveraging venture capital for R&D purposes by resolving regulatory constraints and relaxing immigration restrictions on skilled foreign workers can help alleviate human capital shortages and funding challenges.

The Development of a National Artificial Intelligence (AI) Strategy for SA 

During the 2024/25 period, the NACI, with the approval of the Minister of Science, Technology and Innovation, Professor Bonginkosi Nzimande, led a national process to develop recommendations that can inform the development of a comprehensive national strategy on artificial intelligence (AI). This initiative was undertaken in alignment with the National Development Plan, the White Paper on Science, Technology and Innovation (STI), and its Decadal Plan. NACI partnered with the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) Emerging Digital Technologies for 4IR (EDT4IR) Research Centre for technical support and convened a diverse working group of approximately 75 members, representing academia, civil society, government, industry, and policy. These members contributed expertise across six thematic areas to shape the recommendations. To ensure broad participation and inclusivity, NACI established an Open National AI Strategy Recommendations Forum, inviting all interested stakeholders to engage in the process. Over 12 months, from April 2024 to March 2025, four national workshops were held, producing a clear vision for AI in South Africa, thematic SWOT analyses, and a comparative review of global AI strategies. The resulting draft NACI AI Recommendations Report was presented and discussed at the Inaugural National System of Innovation Transformation Summit in March 2025, with over 300 key stakeholders in attendance. The report was further reviewed by an independent Reference Group and refined for submission as part of NACI’s formal advice to the Minister of Science, Technology and Innovation. 

Building the RDI Information and Cyber Security Capability for South Africa’s National Security 

In 2024/25, the NACI, in collaboration with the CSIR Information and Cybersecurity Centre, led the development of the NACI National Cybersecurity RDI Roadmap. This work responded to the growing urgency for a coordinated national approach to cybersecurity research, development and innovation (RDI). The project aimed to provide strategic recommendations and a roadmap to advise the Minister of Science, Technology and Innovation—Professor Bonginkosi Nzimande— and, through him, Cabinet, on the development of a national cybersecurity RDI strategy. It was undertaken in alignment with the 2019 White Paper on STI and the Foundational Digital Capabilities Research (FDCR) programme, building on NACI’s prior position paper advocating for strengthened cybersecurity RDI within the National System of Innovation (NSI). The roadmap was shaped through a highly consultative process involving experts from academia, government, industry, and civil society. Two focused workshops were held—one with academic and industry experts, and another with government and state actors involved in the National Cybersecurity Policy Framework. These engagements informed draft recommendations and priority focus areas, which were later refined during a final workshop with cross-sectoral stakeholders. The draft roadmap was presented and discussed at the Inaugural NSI Transformation Summit in March 2025, with over 300 participants, including the Minister. It was further reviewed by an independent Reference Group before finalisation. The resulting report offers actionable insights to inform national cybersecurity RDI policy and strengthen South Africa’s innovation ecosystem in the face of evolving digital threats. 18 2024/25 Financial Year • ANNUAL REPORT 

1.1.2  Monitoring and Evaluation 2024 South African Science Technology and Innovation Indicators Report 

NACI launched the 2024 Science, Technology, and Innovation (STI) Indicators Report on 27 August 2024. The Report evaluates the performance of South Africa’s national system of innovation (NSI) based on STI data obtained from reliable local and international sources. Some of the highlights include global research and development (R&D) spending is unevenly distributed, with the United States and China accounting for 28% and 22% of total global R&D, respectively. High-performing countries like Israel and South Korea boast impressive R&D intensities of 4.93% and 4.64%, respectively. Within Africa, Kenya’s R&D intensity of 0.98% surpasses South Africa’s 0.61% as of 2021/22 . In South Africa, R&D expenditure has experienced fluctuations, rising from R22 billion in 2011 to R39 billion in 2017, before declining due to reduced business sector investment up to 2020/21. However, the business sector rebounded with a 26.4% increase in R&D investment in 2021/22, driven by significant investments across various fields and industries. These rebounds highlight the dynamic nature of R&D funding in South Africa, where shifts in business sector investment can have a significant impact. The funding landscape in South Africa is diverse, with government (52.5%, R20.049 billion), business (29.0%, R11.081 billion), and foreign sources (14.5%, R5.539 billion) contributing to R&D funding in 2021/22. Additionally, venture capital investments, targeting key sectors like ICT, Fintech, Food and Beverage, and Manufacturing, accounted for 48.5% of total investment by 2022, but declined by 5.8% in 2021 and 4.4% in 2022. Meanwhile, Nigeria emerged as a leading destination for venture capital investment in Africa, accounting for 22% of total deals in 2022, surpassing South Africa’s 14% share. Infrastructure investments have also played acrucial role in supporting the NSI, with R2.4 billion invested between 2015/16 and 2019/20, including R1.9 billion for astronomy and R504.6 million for nuclear and biodiversity sciences. However, a 20% cut to the National Equipment Programme since 2016/17 has reduced investment in critical research infrastructure. Additionally, South Africa’s digital competitiveness has declined, with the country slipping from 48th in 2019 to 58th in 2023, potentially due to insufficient investment in digital infrastructure, which could hinder the growth of digital industries and innovation Human resources form the backbone of South Africa’s innovation potential, though progress is tempered by gaps. The population with post-secondary education has grown by 0.5% between 2011 and 2020, with half of those aged 20 and above completing secondary education by 2020. University enrolments have climbed 12% from 0.95 million to 1.01 million, and graduates have soared 41% from 165,986 to 233,257 between 2012 and 2021. On the outputs front, South Africa has seen a significant surge in scientific publications, rising from 3,693 in 2000 to 27,208 in 2022, earning a 29th global ranking. However, patenting activity has weakened, with domestic patents declining from 694 in 2019 to 513 in 2022. Conversely, payments for foreign intellectual property have fallen since 2017, while IP sales receipts have increased, with a 6.6% rise in 2021 and a substantial 53% growth in 2022. The economic and social impact of innovation in South Africa is multifaceted, with both challenges and opportunities arising from the country’s evolving landscape. The country’s economy faces headwinds, including a decline in manufacturing output to 524.8 Gross Value Added (GVA) in 2022, alongside high-income inequality (Gini coefficient of 66.92) and a poverty rate of 62.6%. Positive trends have emerged, such as an 11.2% rise in manufacturing exports and a growth in 19 ANNUALREPORT•2024/25 FinancialY ear

The country’s E-Government Development Index score has improved significantly, rising from 0.49 in 2012 to 0.74 in 2022. This progress has propelled South Africa’s global ranking from 101st to 65th out of 193 countries. However, challenges remain, including limited innovation implementation at the municipal level. The NSI Transformation summit 

NACI convened the NSI Transformation Summit at the behest of the Minister Professor Nzimande, at the Birchwood Hotel and OR Tambo Conference Centre on 13 and 14 March 2025. The Summit brought together over 300 delegates, including leaders from government, academic institutions, science councils, business, and organised labour, as well as student and youth representatives, government officials, and other stakeholders. Together, they engaged in robust discussions on critical themes tied to the transformation of the NSI and its pivotal role in fostering democratic, inclusive, and sustainable development for South Africa and its position in an evolving global landscape. Guided by the theme “Science, technology, innovation, and knowledge at the centre of government, education, society, and industry,” the Summit’s presentations and deliberations explored essential dimensions of transformation. Delegates then delved into two pressing questions: • Why has the post-apartheid transformation of the NSI fallen short of its intended goals? • How can we deepen and accelerate this process to ensure science, technology, and knowledge more effectively serve government, education, society, the economy, and the planet? These discussions underscored the urgency of reimagining the NSI as a dynamic catalyst for national progress and global relevance. The Summit adopted a declaration call and numerous recommendations.

1.1.3 Events and stakeholder engagements Launch of the 2024 South African STI Indicators Report 

The National Advisory Council on Innovation (NACI) hosted the launch of the 2024 South African Science, Technology, and Innovation (STI) Indicators Report on 27 August 2024, at the Innovation Hub in Pretoria, with approximately 100 delegates from various sectors, including government, academia, industry, and civil society, in attendance. The Minister Professor Blade Nzimande, delivered the keynote address, highlighting the report’s crucial role in measuring STI’s impact on South Africa’s socio-economic development. Key findings included progress in human resources, research outputs, and digitisation, but also declining R&D investment and venture capital. Discussions emphasised the need for increased funding, collaboration, and effective policymaking, as well as addressing persistent triple challenges of unemployment, poverty and inequality. Minister Professor Blade Nzimande called for a summit to discuss the transformation of the National System of Innovation, emphasising the need for systemic change to drive meaningful progress. 

KZN Roadshow: 2024 South African STI Indicators Report 

NACI partnered with the University of KwaZulu-Natal (UKZN) to host a roadshow at the Durban ICC on 25 November 2024, engaging stakeholders on the key findings of the 2024 South African STI Indicators Report Approximately 50 delegates from government, science councils, universities, and the private sector attended the event. The Report highlighted mixed progress at the national level, with improvements in STI human resources, research excellence, and digital infrastructure investments, but concerns persisted, including low research intensity, declining patent activity, and diminishing venture capital investment. The Report highlighted provincial disparities, with Gauteng, Western Cape, and KwaZulu-Natal leading in R&D expenditure, high-tech jobs, and exports, but facing declining medium-tech employment. Education gaps persisted, with the Free State and KwaZulu-Natal achieving high matric pass rates despite lower GDP per capita. Discussions emphasised the need to foster an innovation culture at the municipal level, remove barriers to innovation, and scale up existing initiatives to drive meaningful change.
