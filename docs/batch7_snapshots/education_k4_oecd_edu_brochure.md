Investing in high quality, evidence-based education is the key to supporting individuals to thrive throughout their lifetimes, and to creating more prosperous, stronger societies. By helping countries understand where, how and why learners of every age develop, we open pathways to success and pave the way for more resilient economies. Education remains the most powerful means to address inequities at their source, sparing us the high cost of social disparities further down the line. The OECD helps governments leverage best practices and access data-driven insights in shaping educational policies for better lives. Our international comparative assessments illuminate the 

similarities and differences among different education systems in OECD countries and beyond. Our education indicators reveal how resources, financing and policies shape learning outcomes. 

In tandem, our thematic reviews of educational systems and policy forums highlight what drives 

teaching excellence, fosters lifelong learning, and prepares citizens for a world where artificial 

intelligence and digital innovations abound. Through initiatives like our skills strategies and policy reviews we help policy makers, educators and communities build strategies that respond to local needs while anticipating global challenges. 

Our forward-looking projects reflect the OECD’s commitment to ensuring that learners are not merely equipped for today, but can flourish in tomorrow’s world. 

Together, we can chart a course where every learner—young or old—has the chance to develop the abilities, attitudes and values that will contribute to a fairer, more prosperous future. The OECD stands ready to help our partners reshape education for the present, and for generations to come.

# How the OECD makes a 

# difference to education 

The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) provides robust comparative data and policy advice and facilitates cooperation to empower governments and stakeholders to strengthen 

educational performance, create effective 

practices, and generate solutions to improve education systems. Our work focuses on what drives equitable and high-quality education: identifying where systems excel, where they struggle, and what strategies can enhance both academic and social outcomes. This evidence-based approach leads to policy insights that help policy makers understand a full spectrum of challenges—ranging from resource allocation through strengthening learning outcomes and the well-being of learners up to the digital transformation of classrooms. The OECD helps countries reimagine the future of education in light of new technologies, demographic shifts, and evolving labour markets, and it accompanies 

them in the difficult process of reform 

implementation. As a result, policy makers gain valuable insights not just into what constitutes success today, but how to equip the next generations to thrive in an ever-changing world. © OECD 2025 5© OECD 2025 6

# What we do 

The best way for education systems to improve is to learn what works in what context from one another. The OECD deploys large-scale surveys and educational policy reviews, designing common methodological and analytical frameworks to compare empirical evidence on nearly all aspects of education systems. The results provide a good indication of what leads to more 

effective and equitable learning and well-being. 

Education at a Glance (EAG) 

Published annually, EAG’s data on more than 100 education indicators gives countries and economies benchmarks on everything from average class sizes and teacher salaries to completion rates and public versus private spending on education. For example, EAG allows policy makers to see how much they invest per student relative to international counterparts, and whether that expenditure is translating into measurable outcomes, such as higher graduation rates or more e q u i t a b l e access.

Countries interested in joining one of the OECD’s 

education surveys can do so by contacting the OECD Directorate for Education and Skills. By joining these 

assessments, countries can contribute to and benefit 

from a global exchange of best practices and evidence-based policy recommendations, enhancing the quality, 

equity and effectiveness of education worldwide. Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 

PISA is the world’s most recognised international student assessment. It measures 15-year-olds’ abilities in reading, mathematics and science, alongside emerging competencies. PISA data has helped more than 100 countries and economies pinpoint policy gaps and learn from one another. For instance, PISA provides breakdowns of performance variations linked to socioeconomic status, and helps governments identify dimensions of learning environment that are linked to better learning and well-being of students. It also lets policy makers track longitudinal trends, illustrating the change in student performance, their learning environment and the organisation of education systems over time. PISA is currently exploring innovative assessments such as the measure of AI and media literacy, and of professional knowledge and employability 

skills in selected occupational fields.

TALIS is the largest international survey of teachers 

and school leaders. TALIS reflects teachers’ perspectives 

on teaching conditions and instructional practices, helping 

countries support teacher retention, teacher effectiveness and address pressing issues like artificial intelligence in classrooms. By 

showing, for example, how collaborative professional environments correlate with higher job satisfaction or better student outcomes, policy makers can target initiatives—such as mentoring programmes 

or peer-learning networks—to support teachers more effectively. 

This helps decision-makers adapt teaching policies, invest in 

teacher training, and refine leadership frameworks in schools 

to strengthen classroom impact and raise the overall quality of education. 

TALIS Starting Strong is the first 

international survey of the early childhood education and care workforce. 55 

countries and territories took part in the latest TALIS round 

OECD Survey on Social and Emotional Skills 

The first international survey that identifies 

and assesses the conditions and practices that shape the social and emotional skills of 10- and 15-year-old students in a comparable way. 

By providing data on, among other things, the link 

between a student’s self-regulation and their academic achievement, policy makers can devise targeted interventions that promote emotional well-being and better academic outcomes.

Survey of Adult Skills (PIAAC) 

PIAAC is the largest international assessment of adult skills. It measures cognitive skills – literacy, numeracy and problem-solving – as well as a range of social and emotional skills, among adults aged 16 to 65. These are key skills needed for individuals to participate in society and for economies to prosper. It also gathers data on how adults use their skills at home and at work, adult learning and socio-emotional skills. PIAAC enables policy-makers to understand how skills are distributed and how they relate to factors such as education (including adult learning), employment, age, or migration background. This helps identify groups for which targeted policy action is needed. In a fast-changing labour market shaped by technological change and demographic pressures, PIAAC provides the data governments 

need to design effective 

policies that build skilled and future-ready workforces. 

International Early Learning and Child Well-Being Study (IELS) 

The world’s first international assessment 

of early learning and child well-being focuses on the cognitive, social and emotional development of 5-year-olds. It enables policy makers to design early learning initiatives that set our youngest citizens on the path to becoming engaged, 

productive and fulfilled 

members of society.

# Provide policy analysis and advice to match educational aspiration to performance 

The OECD mobilises its data and research evidence to advise countries in developing and implementing policies to improve 

their education and skills systems. The OECD’s policy reviews 

range from those on individual national education policies to comparative educational policy and thematic peer analysis. 

These offer countries an opportunity to build deep, cumulative 

and shared learning on key policy dimensions or systems such as the use of resources, evaluation and assessment, equity, the use of digital technologies, the teaching profession, transitions in upper secondary education, early childhood education and care, vocational education and training or higher education. OECD policy reviews are conducted in collaboration with networks of country policy makers and experts, providing multiple opportunities for peer learning among countries. 

The OECD’s policy implementation support provides in-depth 

technical advice and capacity development to ensure planned policies bring about sustainable educational change. Using the OECD Skills Strategy Framework, a tool which helps countries map the dimensions of skills policies, the OECD has 

helped dozens of countries reflect and define the scope of their 

skills strategies. These reviews help countries adopt a strategic, comprehensive approach to enhancing skills development and use across all life stages, while also improving the governance of 

their skills systems. By tailoring the approach to each country’s 

needs, these projects help identify key skills policy priorities and 

offer tailor-made policy recommendations. 

The OECD also provides longer-term, transformative support to national education systems, like our work on Transforming General Upper Secondary Education in Ukraine, where we 

are supporting Ukraine’s ambition that each student in its 

country can pursue his or her own academic trajectory. We are providing Ukraine with international evidence and peer learning to provide the country with a range of policy options to inform its reforms. © OECD 2025 11 National education policy review countries

The OECD’s long-term “leading-edge” thinking looks beyond 

the current state of education to what it can become. Bringing 

together knowledge from diverse fields, the OECD challenges 

policy makers to think about how to leverage innovation to solve societal needs, serving as an international think tank that explores the frontiers and reimagines the future of education. 

For example, the OECD’s work on the future of AI capabilities and 

their educational implications helps policy makers understand 

how AI and robotics are likely to affect work and how education 

should change in anticipation. The project explores which 

human capabilities will be too difficult for AI and robotics to 

reproduce over the next few decades and what education and training will be needed to allow most people to develop the capabilities they need to thrive in an AI world. Our ground-breaking Future of Education and Skills 2040 work considers the types of knowledge, skills, attitudes and values that students and teachers need to thrive in the future. It looks at both learning and teaching and curriculum to inspire policy processes to shape the future of education. Our 2040 project mobilises networks of experts, school leaders and teachers from across the globe to inspire advances in classrooms, schools and policies. Our 21st Century Children work looks at the nature of modern childhood and the ways in which  schools and communities can work together to protect and guide children while still allowing 

them the flexibility to make their own mistakes. By synthesising 

multi-disciplinary research and convening countries to share experiences, the OECD is helping decision-makers shape their policy, practice and research agendas. Our Trends Shaping Education report is a triennial examination of the social, technological, economic, environmental and political forces transforming education systems worldwide. The 

questions raised in Trends are designed to inspire reflection and 

inform strategic thinking on how global trends might transform education and how education can shape a better future. Visionary work about the education systems of tomorrow must be grounded in a thorough and deep understanding 

of the realities of education systems today, and the OECD’s 

future-looking work is informed by in-depth studies like our Digital Education Outlook, a biannual look at how digitalisation is transforming education. The Outlook is a reference for education policy makers and researchers interested in the latest trends and policies internationally related to the increased use of digital technology and data in education.

In addition to data, policy advice and country support, the OECD 

provides a range of tools to make policymaking more efficient and effective and strengthen outcomes. 

The Education Policy Outlook 

The Education Policy Outlook is an analytical observatory that monitors the evolution of policy priorities and developments from early childhood to adult education. It provides acomparative understanding of how policies are evolving, and how they can be best implemented or improved over time. The 

report also tracks policy progress, offering nations a roadmap for refining existing strategies, avoiding ineffective approaches, 

and ensuring that education reforms are both data-driven and sustainable. 

Education GPS 

The Education GPS is an intuitively organised digital platform 

that integrates the directorate’s work, providing easy access to all 

policy relevant analysis, by country or topic, and internationally comparable data on education policies, practices, opportunities and outcomes. By allowing countries to explore and compare data across OECD datasets including PISA, TALIS and EAG, the GPS helps policy makers quickly assess education system performance. 

PISA for Schools 

PISA for Schools provides school-level estimates of performance and information about learning environment to give individual schools a solid evidence base to improve teaching and learning for all students. The questionnaire collects information on 

students’ attitudes towards learning, their school’s learning 

environment, their socio-economic background and their social and emotional skills. © OECD 2025 18
